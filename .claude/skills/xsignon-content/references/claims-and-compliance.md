# Claims and compliance

This is orientation for copywriting, not legal advice. Counsel must review privacy, terms and any regulated claim before launch.

## The rule

Every objective claim must be true and provable before it is published. An objective claim is anything a reader would take as fact: numbers, comparisons, "first", "only", "best", uptime, savings, results, certifications, standards, customers, partners, awards.

Keep a **claims log** (template in `templates.md`): claim, exact wording, source, date of evidence, who verified it, status. A claim with no log row does not ship.

## Safe wording ladder for standards and certifications

Use the strongest line you can prove, never stronger.

| Evidence XSignOn has | Allowed wording |
|---|---|
| Independent audit report issued | "SOC 2 Type II report available on request" (state type and period) |
| Product tested against the standard | "FIDO2 certified" (name the certifying body) |
| Design follows the framework, no certification exists | "Built to align with NIST 800-207" / "Maps to NIST AI-RMF" |
| Supports a customer's own compliance | "Helps you meet GDPR requirements" (never "GDPR compliant" for a product) |
| Nothing provable | Do not mention it |

GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS and SOX are laws or regulations, not certificates. A product cannot "be GDPR compliant" in the way a badge implies. Say what it helps with.

## Testimonials, reviews, case studies

- Only genuine, permitted quotes with the person's name and role as agreed. Never edit meaning.
- Disclose any material connection (paid, partner, employee).
- Case studies need written customer approval for name, logo, figures and wording. Until then: use an anonymised, clearly labelled example ("A regional bank"), and say it is an example, not a result.
- Never present one result as typical. State the method and period.

## Pricing and offers

State the full price, billing period, renewal and cancellation next to the price. No fake urgency, no fake scarcity, no fake "X people viewing". "Free" means free: if it needs a card or converts to paid, say so beside the word.

## Security-specific cautions

- Do not publish technical detail that helps an attacker (internal architecture, versions, unpatched issues, customer environments).
- Do not claim to prevent all attacks. Say what is detected, blocked or contained, and under what conditions.
- Comparisons with named competitors need a method, a date and a source, or they are cut.
- Do not use real customer data, logos or screenshots in demos without permission.

## Regulated and sensitive topics

Health, finance and legal topics: only what a qualified person reviewed; state who and when. Articles that quote healthcare or finance breach costs must cite the original report and year.

## Privacy and consent copy

Real choice, reject as easy as accept, no pre-ticked boxes, state why each form field is needed, unsubscribe in every email. Privacy policy and terms come from counsel, not from the copywriter, and must describe XSignOn's real practices. Never copy another site's policy.

## Images, quotes, licences

Use only images, fonts and text you own or have licensed. Record source and licence. Short attributed quotes are fine; long extracts need permission. Disclose AI-generated images where the platform or law requires, and never present AI output as a real testimonial or review.

## Dark patterns (banned, in copy or flow)

Confirm-shaming, hidden costs, fake urgency, trick wording, forced continuity, pre-checked consent, hard-to-cancel flows, bait and switch.

## Pre-publish legal check

- Every claim has a log row.
- Testimonials permitted and disclosed.
- Prices show total, period, renewal, cancellation.
- Privacy and terms counsel-reviewed and dated.
- Consent offers a real choice.
- Image and font licences recorded.
- No dark patterns.
- Accessibility statement present with standard and contact.
- Regulated claims reviewed by a qualified person.
