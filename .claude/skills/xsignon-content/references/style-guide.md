# Style guide

## Voice and tone

Voice stays the same on every page (see `xsignon-brief.md`). Tone shifts with context, never the facts.

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing page | Confident, specific | "Replace passwords with a fingerprint on a FIDO2 key." |
| Help page | Patient, step by step | "First, open Settings. Then select Security." |
| Error message | Calm, specific | "That code has expired. Request a new one." |
| Legal | Precise, neutral | "We collect your work email to send your report." |
| Empty state | Brief, encouraging | "No reports yet. Run your first check." |

## Plain language

- Use the word the reader uses. "Sign in", not "authenticate" (unless the reader is technical and the term is defined).
- Short common words: use, help, start, show, set up. Not utilize, facilitate, commence.
- Define jargon on first use or cut it. Spell out abbreviations once.
- Second person for offers and steps ("you"). "We" only for actions XSignOn takes.
- Contractions are fine in marketing and help copy ("you'll"). Use full forms in legal text.
- Reading level: homepage and help grade 6 to 8; technical product copy grade 8 to 10; legal summary grade 8.
- Read aloud. Rewrite any sentence you stumble on.

## The ten sentence rules (apply in order when editing)

1. One idea per sentence. Split sentences joined by "and" that carry two claims.
2. Aim for 15 to 20 words. Over 25: split or cut.
3. Subject, verb, object. "We confirm your booking", not "Confirmation will be sent."
4. Active voice. Passive only when the actor is unknown, does not matter, or naming it blames the reader.
5. Front-load the point. Put the answer or key word first in the sentence, paragraph and section.
6. Concrete nouns, strong verbs. "We help you book", not "We provide assistance with booking."
7. Turn -tion, -ment, -ance nouns back into verbs where clearer. "Confirm", not "confirmation".
8. Parallel structure in lists. All verbs, all nouns, or all full sentences.
9. Transitions earn their place. Use first, then, for example, but, so. Cut "furthermore", "moreover", "it is important to note".
10. Vary rhythm. Short sentence after a long one. Never three long sentences in a row.

## Paragraphs, lists, headings

- One idea per paragraph, 2 to 4 sentences. More than 4 lines on a phone: split.
- Open with the point. No throat-clearing ("In today's world", "When it comes to").
- Lists for 3 or more parallel items. Numbered for steps. Introduce every list with a lead-in. Items under two lines.
- One H1 (the promise or answer). H2 as answers or outcomes, not labels. H3 under H2. Never skip a level.
- Read only the headings: they should tell the story.

## House mechanics (use exactly)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Spelling | US English: organize, color, center, license (noun and verb) |
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headings and buttons. Proper names keep their capitals. |
| Serial comma | Yes |
| Dashes | No em dashes in web copy. Comma, colon or full stop instead. Hyphen for compounds and ranges. |
| Numbers | Spell out one to nine in body copy, numerals for 10 and above; numerals always for prices, dates, versions, measurements |
| Dates | March 4, 2026. Never 04/03/2026. |
| Times | 2:00 p.m. CT, with time zone when it matters |
| Percent | 20% in body copy |
| Currency | $20, state what it covers |
| Abbreviations | Spell out once, then short form |
| Links | 2 to 6 words, descriptive |
| Product names | Exactly as in `xsignon-brief.md` |
| Quotes | Straight or curly, but one style across the site |

## Weak words swap

| Weak | Replace with |
|---|---|
| innovative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art | the specific feature and what it does |
| seamless, effortless | the steps and the time ("3 steps, about 2 minutes") |
| world-class, best-in-class | evidence: rating, source, date |
| robust, comprehensive, powerful | what is included; a number |
| solutions (alone) | the named product or service |
| leverage, utilize, facilitate | use, help, set up |
| very, really, extremely | cut, or give the measure |
| in order to / due to the fact that / at this point in time | to / because / now |
| please do not hesitate to contact us | "Email us at info@xsignon.com." |
| click here / read more | descriptive link text |
| holistic, end-to-end, synergy, paradigm | say the actual thing |

AI tells to remove: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, ever-evolving, "in today's fast-paced world", triple adjectives, em-dash stacking, vague superlatives, any sentence that could describe any company.

## Before and after (security examples)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| We leverage cutting-edge AI to deliver seamless protection. | PostureOne shows your identity, data and AI risk in one view. |
| Our robust solution ensures 100% security. | Segmentation limits how far an attacker can move inside your network. |
| Click here for more information. | See how PostureOne works. |
| Passwords are the weakest link and hackers are coming. | Stolen passwords cause most account takeovers. A fingerprint key cannot be phished. (cite the source) |
| Invalid input. | Enter a work email, like name@company.com. |
| Your request has been received. | Message sent. We reply within 1 business day. [CHECK: confirm response time] |

## Reach: write for as many people as possible

- **Plain, translatable language.** No idioms ("ballpark", "low-hanging fruit"), slang, sports metaphors or culture-bound jokes. Short sentences. Full-form dates and numbers. Leave room in buttons for text that grows 30% in translation.
- **Readers on phones.** Short paragraphs, descriptive headings, key facts in text.
- **Screen readers.** Descriptive links, alt text that states meaning, headings in order, no information only in images or colour.
- **Cognitive and low-literacy readers, second-language readers.** Grade 6 to 8 where possible, one idea per sentence, define terms where they appear, repeat key terms rather than switching synonyms.
- **No assumptions.** Do not assume gender, age, ability, role, team size or location. Prefer "they" or rewrite to "you".
- **Security literacy varies.** Explain a term once in plain words before using it. Keep a plain summary above technical detail.
- **Global audience.** Avoid US-only references unless the page is US-only. State time zones. State currency.

## Inclusive language swap

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| manpower, man hours | staff, work hours |
| guys (mixed group) | team, everyone |
| he/she as default | they, or "you" |
| chairman, spokesman | chair, spokesperson |
| the disabled | people with disabilities |
| crazy, insane, dumb (for ideas) | surprising, unclear, risky |
| grandfathered | exempt, legacy |
| blacklist / whitelist | blocklist / allowlist |
| master / slave | primary / replica |
| normal users | typical users |
| sanity check | quick check |
| dummy value | placeholder value |

## Microcopy

- **Buttons:** verb plus outcome, 2 to 5 words, sentence case. "Book a strategy call", "Download the guide", "Send message". State cost or commitment beside the button when money or time is involved.
- **Forms:** label above the field (a noun), helper text only where the format is unclear, "Required" in words, not colour alone.
- **Errors:** what happened, why, how to fix it, beside the field. Never "Invalid input". Never blame.
- **Empty state:** what is empty, why, what to do next, one action.
- **Confirmation:** what happened, what happens next, when.
- **404:** say the page was not found, offer search, home and contact. No jokes that hide the way back.
- **Cookie banner:** plain words, Accept and Reject equally easy.
